## Normal distribution and standardization

A normal random variable (X) has mean (\mu), variance (\sigma^2), and values ranging from (-\infty) to (+\infty); its variance must be greater than zero.

Convert (X) to the standard normal variable (Z) using (Z=(X-\mu)/\sigma), where (\sigma) is the standard deviation.

Example parameters: mean (50), variance (9), so the standard deviation is (3). Thus (X=53) converts to (Z=1).

The normal distribution is symmetric around zero after standardization; its mean, median, and mode coincide. Each side of zero contains probability (0.5).

## Reading the standard normal table

The table used in the examples gives the area between (Z=0) and a positive (Z)-value.

Locate the first decimal place in the row and the second decimal place in the column; for example, (Z=1.25) uses row (1.2) and column (0.05).

The area from (Z=0) to (Z=1) is (0.3413). Symmetry gives the same area from (Z=-1) to (Z=0).

## Calculating tail and interval probabilities

For (X>53), standardize to (Z>1): (P(Z>1)=0.5-0.3413=0.1584).

For (X<53), use (P(Z<1)=0.5+0.3413=0.841), as stated in the lecture.

For (X>47), standardize to (Z>-1). By symmetry, the area from (-1) to (0) is (0.3413), giving (0.5+0.3413=0.841).

For (X<47), standardize to (Z<-1): (0.5-0.3413=0.158).

For (47<X<53), the standardized interval is (-1<Z<1). Add the equal areas on either side of zero: (2(0.3413)=0.6826).

For (53<X<56), standardize the bounds to (1<Z<2). Subtract the table area from (0) to (1) from the area from (0) to (2); the lecture gives these as (0.341) and (0.477), respectively.

For (X>56), standardize to (Z>2); subtract the area from (0) to (2) from (0.5).

For probabilities below a positive (Z)-value, combine the left half of the curve ((0.5)) with the table area from zero to that value. For intervals between two positive values, subtract their zero-to-value table areas.
