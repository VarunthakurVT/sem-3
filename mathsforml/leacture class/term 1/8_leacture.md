## Variance and Its Relationship with Standard Deviation

Variance is the arithmetic mean of the squared deviations of observations from their mean: [ \sigma^2=\frac{\sum (X-\bar X)^2}{n} ]

Standard deviation is denoted by (\sigma), while variance is denoted by (\sigma^2)

Standard deviation and variance are related as: [ \text{Variance}=(\text{Standard Deviation})^2 ] [ \text{Standard Deviation}=\sqrt{\text{Variance}} ]

A standard deviation of (9) gives a variance of (81)

A variance of (9) gives a standard deviation of (3)

The lecture clarified that variance does not use the square root, while standard deviation does

Variance is always non-negative because squared deviations cannot be negative

Variance becomes (0) only when every observation is equal to the mean, meaning all observations are identical; for example, repeated values of (5) produce zero deviations

## Unit Differences and Coefficients of Dispersion

Direct comparison is not appropriate when two series use different units, such as height in centimetres and weight in kilograms

Measures of central tendency and dispersion normally retain the original unit of the data

Coefficients of dispersion cancel the units and produce unit-free ratios

The main coefficients introduced were based on range, quartile deviation, mean deviation, and standard deviation

**Coefficient based on range**:  
[  
\frac{\text{Largest value}-\text{Smallest value}}  
{\text{Largest value}+\text{Smallest value}}  
]

For the data set (1, 6, 2, 3, 0, 8), the range is:  
[  
8-0=8  
]

**Coefficient based on quartile deviation**:  
[  
\frac{Q_3-Q_1}{Q_3+Q_1}  
]

Quartiles divide data into four equal parts, with (Q_1), (Q_2), and (Q_3) used to describe the divisions

**Coefficient based on mean deviation**:  
[  
\frac{\text{Mean Deviation}}{\text{Average used to calculate mean deviation}}  
]

The denominator depends on the reference average:

Mean deviation from mean: mean deviation divided by mean

Mean deviation from median: mean deviation divided by median

Mean deviation from mode: mean deviation divided by mode

The same principle applies when geometric mean or harmonic mean is used

**Coefficient based on standard deviation**:  
[  
\frac{\text{Standard Deviation}}{\text{Mean}}  
]

These coefficients are useful for comparing variability in series measured using different units

## Coefficient of Variation and Consistency Comparison

Coefficient of variation is abbreviated as (CV)

Its formula is:  
[  
CV=\frac{\text{Standard Deviation}}{\text{Mean}}\times100  
]

Coefficient of variation expresses relative variation as a percentage

The result is unit-free before multiplication by (100), and is represented as a percentage afterward

Standard deviation must be used in the formula, not variance; if variance is provided, first calculate its square root

**Boys’ weight data**:

Mean weight: (50) kg

Variance: (25)

Standard deviation: [ \sqrt{25}=5 ]

Coefficient of variation: [ \frac{5}{50}\times100=10% ]

**Girls’ height data**:

Mean height: (150) cm

Variance: (64)

Standard deviation: [ \sqrt{64}=8 ]

Coefficient of variation: [ \frac{8}{150}\times100\approx5.33% ]

The girls’ series is more consistent because its coefficient of variation is lower

A lower (CV) means less relative variability and greater consistency

A higher (CV) means more relative variability and lower consistency

In this example, the boys’ data has (10%) variation, while the girls’ data has approximately (5.33%)

## Key Revision Points

Variance is the square of standard deviation

Standard deviation is the square root of variance

Variance equals zero only when all observations are the same

Coefficients of dispersion are unit-free

(CV=\frac{SD}{Mean}\times100)

Lower (CV) means more consistency

Always convert variance to standard deviation before calculating coefficient of variation
