- Critical outcomes:
    - Deterministic experiments have one predictable outcome; probabilistic experiments have two or more possible outcomes

## Deterministic and Probabilistic Experiments

- Deterministic experiments produce a single, predictable outcome under the given conditions
- Pressure–volume behavior and the equation \(v = u + at\) were used as examples of predictable relationships
- Probabilistic experiments have two or more possible outcomes, so the exact result cannot be known in advance
- Tossing a coin, with possible outcomes of head or tail, was used as the main probabilistic example
- Passing or failing an examination was given as another example of a probabilistic outcome

## Standard Trials and Events

- A standard experiment is conducted under homogeneous and identical conditions
- Coin tossing, dice throwing, and appearing in an examination were presented as standard trials
- An event is an outcome or case resulting from a trial
- Coin tossing produces the events head and tail
- Throwing a die produces the events \(1, 2, 3, 4, 5,\) and \(6\)

## Exhaustive Events and Counting Outcomes

- Exhaustive events include all possible outcomes of a trial
- One coin has two exhaustive outcomes: head and tail
- Two coins have four outcomes: \(HH, HT, TH,\) and \(TT\)
- Three coins have eight outcomes, generated by attaching head and tail to each of the four two-coin outcomes
- Four coins have sixteen outcomes
- The general formula for \(N\) simultaneously tossed coins is \(2^N\)
- One die has six outcomes
- Two simultaneously thrown dice have \(6 \times 6 = 36\) possible ordered combinations
- The general formula for \(N\) simultaneously thrown dice is \(6^N\)

## Favorable Events and “Exactly,” “At Most,” and “At Least”

- Favorable events are the outcomes responsible for the occurrence of the event being considered
- For three coin tosses, exactly one head is favorable in three outcomes: \(HTT, THT,\) and \(TTH\)
- “At most one head” means zero or one head; for three coin tosses, the favorable count is four
- “At least one head” includes one, two, or three heads; among eight three-coin outcomes, the count is seven
- “At most two heads” includes zero, one, or two heads and excludes the outcome with three heads
- “At least two heads” includes outcomes with two or three heads; the class discussion counted four such outcomes for three coin tosses
- Several counting examples caused confusion because “at most” includes all smaller values, while “at least” includes the stated value and every larger value

## Equally Likely and Mutually Exclusive Events

- Outcomes are equally likely when there is no reason to expect one outcome more often than another
- Head and tail in an ideal coin toss were treated as equally likely outcomes
- Mutually exclusive events cannot occur simultaneously
- If one event occurs, it excludes the occurrence of the other mutually exclusive events
- A complete probability model requires mutually exclusive, exhaustive, and equally likely outcomes

## Classical Probability and Complementary Probability

- If \(m\) outcomes are favorable out of \(n\) exhaustive outcomes, the probability is:

\[

P(E)=\frac{m}{n}

\]

- The probability of an event not occurring is:

\[

P(E')=\frac{n-m}{n}=1-\frac{m}{n}=1-P(E)

\]

- The probabilities of an event and its non-occurrence add to one:

\[

P(E)+P(E')=1

\]

- Every probability lies between 0 and 1:

\[

0\leq P(E)\leq 1

\]

- \(P(E)=0\) represents an impossible event
- \(P(E)=1\) represents a sure event
