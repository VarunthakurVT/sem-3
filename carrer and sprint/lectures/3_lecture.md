## Percentage Change Using Approximation

- A revenue change from `147.8` to `108.1` was identified as `39.5`.
- The percentage change was estimated by comparing `39.5` with the original value `147.8`.
- Since `10%` of `147.8` equals `14.78`, the change was close to three times that amount.
- The resulting percentage was estimated at approximately `27%`, rather than requiring lengthy exact division.
- The shortcut depends on recognizing convenient multiples of the denominator and placing the answer in a sensible range.

## Identifying the Highest Per-Coin Production Cost

## Introduction to Bar Charts and Histograms

- A bar chart was defined as a method for visualizing data using rectangular bars.
- Bar charts support comparisons, such as comparing the marks of students or values across branches and years.
- Histograms were distinguished by their use of class intervals, such as:
    - `50 to 60`
    - `60 to 70`
    - `70 to 80`
    - `80 to 100`
- In a histogram:
    - The width of a rectangle represents the class interval.
    - The height represents frequency, or the number of observations in that interval.
- A bar chart was presented as a related form of visualization that generally uses individual categories or values rather than histogram-style class intervals.
